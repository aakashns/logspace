A year ago I started posting a short update every week about what I was working on. No launch plan, no audience to speak of, just a habit. Here's what I learned.

## Small releases beat big launches

Every time I saved something up for a big reveal, it landed with a thud. The things that got real feedback were the small, slightly embarrassing releases I shipped on a Tuesday afternoon.

Shipping small has a compounding effect:

- You find out sooner when you're wrong.
- Each release is easy to explain in a sentence or two.
- The people following along feel like they're part of it.

![My desk on a typical Tuesday](desk.jpg)

## Writing it down makes the work better

Having to describe the week's progress forced me to notice when there wasn't any. It also made me a much better judge of what was worth doing next.

> If you can't explain what you did this week in three sentences, you probably did too many things.

![Flipping through a year of notes](notebook.mp4)

## Share before it feels ready

The best conversations I had all year started with a half-finished idea. People are far more willing to help with something that's clearly still in progress.

If you've been thinking about building in public, start this week. Keep it small, and keep going.
